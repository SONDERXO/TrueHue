# RGB-channel exposure limit assessment

The previous luminance limit could leave the full positive exposure gain in place even when a colour channel saturated. This assessment tested one RGB8 limit on 38 already available development cases. It used the saved input, masks and controls, including one original-size local portrait. The candidate keeps a common exposure gain for the image and uses the existing renderer. It reduces the gain when that gain would newly saturate an RGB channel on measured, unprotected facial pixels in the application support. The comparison uses the existing colour correction at unit exposure. The rule preserves the gamma and colour controls and uses bisection to find the largest safe gain at the quantization boundary.

The screen required lower clipping on the problem cases, exact preservation of two useful lighting examples and 11 identities, and preserved reference recovery on known underexposure cases. It passed eight generated tests. On the saved portrait and both flagged development cases, clipping decreased. All protected and outside-support pixel comparisons passed and all 11 identity outputs matched exactly. One of the two useful lighting examples changed. The 11 combined-underexposure outputs matched exactly. Four of 11 pure-underexposure outputs changed and mean RGB error against the reference increased from 2.38 to 2.56 code levels. Mean CIEDE2000 decreased from 1.333 to 1.325. Existing gains relative to uncorrected inputs persisted on these development checks.

The candidate failed the screen. A common gain can reduce exposure throughout the selected region when highlights at only a few locations hit the limit. The evidence supports a targeted highlight treatment as the mechanism to investigate. The report covers a research comparison of the frozen WB314 outputs and does not authorize a deployed policy or a new evaluation round. Images, per-case measurements and reviewer responses are kept in private local outputs.

The assessment code is in `scripts/assess_wb314_rgb_exposure_headroom.py`, with generated coverage in `tests/test_rgb_exposure_headroom.py`.
