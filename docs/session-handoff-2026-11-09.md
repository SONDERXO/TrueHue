## What the system does

- Detects a face and selects usable skin regions.
- Measures skin colour using Lab and ITA, and estimates a continuous Monk Skin Tone (MST) score.
- Predicts brightness/gamma, red gain and blue gain.
- Decides whether to correct an image or leave it unchanged.
- Protects excluded objects and facial regions. For transparent prescription glasses, the intended behaviour is to exclude frames and rims while retaining visible skin through lenses.
- Has a Python desktop demonstration and an older Android prototype. Current masking was brought into the desktop demonstration; newer experimental correction candidates have not been adopted for runtime use. Existing Android and demo changes are uncommitted and must be preserved.

## Limitations and restrictions to carry forward

- Small cohorts; repeated use of exposed groups. Publisher groups are not verified identities across datasets.
- Repeated conditions and folds are not independent people or photos. Constraint counts are often repeated occurrences.
- Synthetic distortions and studio/chart backgrounds do not represent all real lighting. Scene cues may be acquisition-specific.
- RGB reconstruction error is not perceived naturalness or proof of true skin colour. Mask failures, contamination, missed skin, beard/glasses uncertainty, clipping and hard boundaries remain relevant.
- Some older experiments borrowed original masks; do not mix their claims with actual-input masking results.
