# Local exposure attenuation at clipped highlights

The image-wide RGB limit reduced clipping but also reduced useful exposure recovery. This follow-up attenuates the positive exposure contribution at individual measured, unprotected facial pixels. It considers pixels that reach a full RGB8 channel under the saved result and stay below that level in both the input and the same colour correction at unit exposure. The smallest attenuation that reaches the nearest quantization boundary is applied along the rendered RGB contribution. The saved result supplies every other pixel.

The new rule passed three generated checks and all 43 related tests. The assessment reused 38 saved development cases with one original-size portrait. It passed the predeclared technical checks for clipping, reference recovery, original dimensions and exact preservation outside targeted highlights. The two development cases with reported excessive brightening had fewer full-channel pixels. Of the two examples with useful brightening, one matched exactly and the other only changed at already clipped highlights. All 11 identities and 11 combined-underexposure outputs matched exactly. The 11 pure-underexposure cases retained their improvements compared with their inputs. Mean RGB and CIEDE2000 reference errors were essentially unchanged.

The original-size portrait showed substantially fewer fully saturated pixels, with a small change in median luminance. A clipped channel often moved by just one RGB code level, so the saturation count alone offers limited evidence of visible improvement. The private comparison presents the original, the frozen result and the trial at matching sizes for appearance review. The candidate is at that review stage. These are development observations using the frozen WB314 controls and saved support.
